## Kindling v0.9.0: motivations, run data and new inventions

Three bodies of work since v0.8.0 (92 commits), each step checked with 10 seeds x 500 years on the standard map and, for most inventions steps, 10 seeds x 750 years on 800x800. Full logs: docs/PROGRESS-v9.md, docs/PROGRESS-data.md, docs/PROGRESS-inventions.md.

**Headline:** people now have reasons to move, marry, quarrel and seek standing. Runs write structured data, and saves are about half the size. After iron and sail come pitch and sealed jars, provisions, pilotage and sky lore, ships, iron farming, steel at the smith's hearth, tallies and records, charts, feasts and ale, native gold cast in moulds, and a recipe realism pass. Save format 14; v0.8.0 saves still load.

### Honest measurement and no walking on water (v9 step 1)
- Every landfall is logged: by sea, on foot or adrift, where from, and how wide the open-water gap was. The report now shows how each continent was reached. (This revealed that one v0.8.0 "crossing" in seed 1 was really on foot.)
- Nobody walks across a strait any more. Scouting, moves and daughter bands need an all-land line.
- Founders start on a continent at least 6 cells from any other landmass. **This changes which world each seed gives.**

### Human motivations (v9 steps 2-8)
- **Rumours fade.** Word of a far shore fades after 50 years unless it is seen again. Look-outs keep watching, and rumours pass only to neighbours whose boats could use them.
- **Seamanship is lived and taught.** It grows from voyages, scouting and visits overseas, and bands teach it to coastal neighbours at meetings.
- **Dissent.** Hunger, crowding and pressure build dissent, so crowded bands split early. Those who leave in anger travel further, take ship more often and carry heritable *ambition* with them. Ambition widens a voyage's reach and stiffens resolve in a confrontation.
- **Wanderlust.** Curious, young bands are drawn to land nobody has camped on lately, and to a known far shore.
- **Intermarriage.** At peaceful meetings bands exchange spouses, which mixes their ancestry, forms marriage ties and halves grudges. In-laws rarely fight.
- **Refinement.** Each recipe has a quality that improves with practice and is taught at half strength. Better quality means better boats, yields and protection.
- **Prestige.** Bands earn standing and it fades over time. It biases who learns from whom and who marries whom, and draws ambitious bands to sea. It has no direct effect on war or growth.

### Pitch, jars and provisions (inventions steps 1-2)
- **Pitch** (new material) is boiled from **resinous wood** (pine, birch; new material, found where there is real woodland in taiga, temperate forest, mountain, steppe and tundra) in a fired pot. It caulks hulls (0.6x upkeep) and glues hafts.
- **Sealed jars** (new form) are a fired pot sealed with pitch. Stored food spoils 0.8x as fast and the store holds more.
- **Drying racks** work where the air is dry or the winter freezes, and **cold cellars** work for settled people in hard winters. Both cut spoilage.
- **Endurance at sea** is how far a crew's food and water last: 18 cells for everyone, more with food that keeps, water skins and jars. Past it, losses rise steeply. Short hops are unchanged.

### Finding the way (steps 3-4)
- **Pilotage** is learned out of sight of land. Crews read signs of land up to 10 x pilot cells away and steer toward it, without extra range. Routes are remembered up to 3x longer and passed on further.
- **Sky lore** is one practice for farmers and sailors. It gives farmers up to +5% sowing effort (about +1-1.5% in practice). At sea it narrows heading error, but only for crews with pilotage.

### Ships and expeditions (steps 5, 5b)
- **Ships** (new form) are a plank boat, pitch and a metal point, put together at the water. They give 1.4x endurance (more with quality) and 1.25x range with a usable sail. They add no seaworthiness.
- **Planned expeditions:** a well-stocked ship crew sails as far as its endurance on a planned voyage.
- **Woven mat sails** need a plank hull or a ship. Long ship legs teach seamanship and pilotage faster.

### Iron in the fields (step 6)
- Iron axes and edges open forest and heavy wet ground for farming and plough 1.15x. Opened ground stays open for everyone and slowly fades where it is abandoned.
- On 800x800: field cells +5%, population +8%, disease +8%, war unchanged.

### Steel (steps 7, 13)
- **Steel** (new material) is made from a hot wrought-iron bar and a hard hammer over an open charcoal hearth with bellows, quenched at water (p 0.03). The same hearth also forges iron and steel edges and points.
- Steel knives, points, spears and axes are the sharpest, longest-lasting tools. Fights get deadlier only through better edges (about +5% losses inflicted), with no flat multiplier.
- At the last check, steel appeared in 8 of 9 living worlds on 800x800 (median Y373) and 2 of 10 on the standard map.

### Tallies, records and ties (steps 8, 8b)
- **Marks** (new form): a notched tally, a clay token or a fired token.
- **Records** grow only where there is something to count: a big store, several strong ties, a herd or tribute owed. At full records, spoilage is 5% lower, recipes are forgotten 25% more slowly and ties last longer.
- **The 8-partner cap is gone.** Ties fade unless they are kept up. Each year a band's care goes mostly to its strongest ties (trade, marriage, kinship), so it keeps about two **close ties**. Loose ties fade but linger, and records widen the care budget. Partners per band now average about 18-31 (max 85 at the checks).

### Charts (step 9)
- Bands with records and pilotage both at 0.4+ and a boat keep **charts**. Charted routes don't fade, and a chart teaches routes further and more often.
- Charts are a minority thing: 8/10 worlds on 800x800 (first Y447-Y706), 2/10 on the standard map.

### Feasts, ale and standing (steps 10, 10b)
- **Feasts:** a band with surplus food hosts a neighbour and gives part of it. A feast can be as big as the larger of the two bands. Standing now comes from food given (feasts and food to the hungry), not from spare labour.
- **Ale** is a sealed jar of grain at the water, brewed by grain farmers in warm country. It adds a little food and doubles a feast's standing.
- Standing from ornaments grows with band size (x sqrt(pop/25)).
- Feasts are everywhere early (by about Y66), ale comes late, and no single band becomes a hub.

### Native gold (steps 11, 11b)
- **Gold** (new material) is a rare map resource: quartz veins in the hills and placer gold in the rivers below them, about 0.1-0.3% of cells. It comes from map generator 5 and leaves every other map layer unchanged.
- **Gold rings are cast:** a clay mould (cut, then fired), a crucible of gold (raw gold in a fired pot), and the closed charcoal furnace that smelts copper. There is no cold-hammered gold. Moulds and crucibles are new forms.
- Rings give the most standing of any ornament (about 1.0-1.2x the mean prestige for wearers at the last checks). Gold is never traded.

### Recipe realism (steps 12, 12b, 13)
- Only wood is carved into shafts, and only wooden shafts become spears.
- Tin makes no edges, points, needles or nails.
- Copper is still cold-hammered. Bronze tools are cast (crucible and fired mould). Iron and steel are hot-forged with a hard hammer.
- Bronze from the two metals needs copper-melting heat and a crucible. Bronze from the two ores is unchanged.
- **Cloth** is woven from linen or wool yarn on a **loom** (both new forms), and garments are sewn from cloth. Sails are made from cloth (or mats) and work only on plank hulls and ships.
- Obsidian pendants are ground, not hammered.
- Renames: "rawhide-strung bow", "sherd-lined kiln".
- Experiments now reach for what a route needs (the tar pot, the forge hammer, mast and sail, fresh pitch on a fired pot), so pitch and jars are no longer held back by the experiment picker. Resinous wood now draws the same attention as plain wood.

### Run data and saves (data groundwork)
- `--out DIR [--out-genealogy]` writes, per seed, `manifest.json` (including the git commit), `metrics.csv` (every yearly stat and counter), `summary.json`, `chronicle.ndjson` and `frames.ndjson`, plus `genealogy.csv` if asked. Schema v1 is in docs/DATA_SCHEMA.md, and writing these outputs never changes a run. `--save` accepts `{seed}` in the file name.
- **Saves are about 45-55% of their old size** on big worlds (a 147 MB save becomes 83 MB) and save about twice as fast. They are still lossless.
- The event log is split into three rings, so on an 800x800 world it reaches about 8-17 years back instead of less than one season. A history store keeps yearly frames (decadal before the last 100 years) and the full genealogy. The band log in the desktop UI uses the genealogy for older events.
- New major events: `LongestCrossing`, `FirstShip` (first crossing by ship), `FirstRecords`, `FirstChart`, `FirstFeast`. History-store-only events: `Married`, `Feast`. The new counters and YearStats columns for every step are listed in docs/DATA_SCHEMA.md.
- Tools: `tools/collect_runs.py`. `tools/agg_results.py` reads text reports or `--out` directories. CI run outputs are archived as `run-<id>` pre-releases (docs/CI-ARCHIVES.md).

### Balance (10 seeds x 500 years, standard map, vs v0.8.0)
- Population 1096051 (v0.8.0: 891377).
- War deaths 0.392%/yr (0.356). Disease deaths 0.117%/yr (0.111).
- 9 seeds take up farming (9).
- Second continent reached by boat in 4 of 10 seeds, first at Y114, none before Y100.
- Items 74.1 (66.4). Discovery-order tau 0.16 (0.134). Mean largest lineage 26.4% (27.4%).
- 800x800, 750 years (9 living worlds; seed 499246298 dies out early, as in every build): population 7.74M, war 0.294%/yr, disease 0.177%/yr, 1/10 crossed (Y632).

### Compatibility
- **Save format 14.** Saves from v0.8.0 (format 8) and older (formats 1-7) still load: new materials, practices and fields are filled in neutrally. Older builds can't open v0.9.0 saves.
- On every load, recipes the new physics forbid are retired and dropped (`clean_recipes`), for example tin blades and old furnace steel, and kits are re-derived. Bands keep the steel and gold they hold. Pitch from plain wood is re-pointed to resinous wood. Cold-hammered gold rings from early step-11 saves are kept but never taught. The first re-save after loading such a save differs slightly in season figures, and later re-saves are identical.
- **Old maps get no gold.** Saved maps keep generator 4. Only newly generated worlds (generator 5) have gold.
- **Seed histories differ from v0.8.0**, because of the new starting rule and every new material and recipe. Same seed, different world history.
- Native and web runs of the same save diverge, as before (float maths differs between targets).

### Known limitations
- **Long crossings stay rare:** about 1 in 10 worlds on 800x800 over 750 years, and 4 in 10 on the standard map. Most unreached continents need 47-171-cell legs, beyond the best ships. This rate is accepted for now: crossings are a guard rail, not a target.
- **Prestige tracks band size only weakly** (r about 0.28-0.30 at the last checks, against a 0.4-0.6 goal); no further tuning in this release.
- **Ale and ships are rare:** ale in 1/10 standard and 2/9 big worlds, ships in 0/10 and 1/9. Each needs pitch, jars and more in one band.
- Gold rings are rare on the standard map (1/10).
- Pitch comes late on big maps (median Y265), because people reach pine and birch country late. This is geography and has not been tuned.
- Disease still rises with farming and crowding. Wells and drains are left for later.

### Usage
```
kindling --seed 8 --threads 4 --map 1650x1650
kindling --headless --seeds 1..10 --years 500 --threads 4 [--out out/data] [--save 'out/s{seed}.civsave']
```
Web: unzip kindling-web.zip and serve the `web-dist/` folder over HTTP.
